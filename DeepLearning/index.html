DEEP LEARNING

In search of the way brain works ...

After getting mesmerized by deep learning in 2019, I've decided to build a good foundation on it, which led to a tremendous effort in learning and coding (You can see part of it in the following sections). I've also changed my master thesis subject from IoT to deep learning (as much as possible) and done some good research with significant results. Following is a brief description of it.

One effective method to improve coding algorithms in the transmission of messages is obtained by paying more attention to the amount of information contained in each part of the message and transmit information accordingly. Based on this simple but practical idea, we proposed two methods that can significantly impact the performance of the existing coding algorithms. As you can see in the chart, they were able to reduce bitrate by even more than 25% while achieving the same quality as before.

Im thinking:

As mother nature made an amazing organ named the brain over millions of years, what we have today is absolutely not enough. I mean, look how generalizable, fast, adaptable, effective it is. It even works with slow chemical reactions between neurons, not the fast computations done by GPUs. Even in deep learning, we carefully design the structure for a specific task and use derivatives in our optimization algorithms, not fair to the brain, I think.

"I have always been convinced that the only way to get artificial intelligence to work is to do the computation in a way similar to the human brain. That is the goal I have been pursuing. We are making progress, though we still have lots to learn about how the brain actually works."
- Geoffrey Hinton

LEARNING PATH

Changing the connections

Sometimes having lots of learning sources makes confusion, and that's true about deep learning. My solution was to keep reading or watching several articles, books, and videos. As a result, I learned and (probably) coded in these subjects of deep learning:

Basic: Layers (Dense, BatchNorm, Dropout, ...) - Regularization Techniques (Dropout, BatchNorm, Data augmentation, DropConnect, ...) - Loss functions (Hinge loss, Cross Entropy losses, MSE, MAE, ...) - Optimization algorithms (SGD, SGD+Momentum, Nestrov momentum, Adagrad, RMSProp, Adam) - Preprocessing
Computer Vision: Layers (Conv., TransConv., GDN, IGDN, Pooling, ...)
Recurrent Neural Networks: Layers (Simple RNN, LSTM, GRU, Conv. LSTM, Bidirectionals)
Generative Adversarial Networks: CGAN - DCGAN - CycleGAN - ...
Reinforcement Learning: Essentials
Network Structures: AE (AE, VAE, CAE, ...) - Classification (AlexNet, VGG, GoogLeNet, ResNe(x)t, NiN, FractalNet, DenseNet, SqueezeNet, ...) - Detection ((Fast(er)) R-CNN, SPPNet, YOLO, ...) - Segmentation (Mask R-CNN, SegNet, U-Net, ...)
Applications: Face detection and recognition - NLP and translation - Telecommunication coding - Data generation - Style transfer - Word embedding - ...
Etc.

Through this process, I've found the following sources so helpful.

CS231n (Stanford)


Probably the best learning source for me was videos of Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition (CS231n), held at Stanford University. I've seen both 2016 and 2017's course videos and, although Andrej Karpathy starred at the first one, 2017's was just more interesting. I just loved how beautiful and intuitive they explained the subjects. Hats off to them.

Coursera


One of the famous learning sources available on the internet is Deep Learning Specialization instructed by Andrew NG, one of the pioneers in deep learning, available at Coursera. It includes five courses about deep learning titled: Neural Networks and Deep Learning - Improving Deep Neural Networks - Structuring Machine Learning Projects - Convolutional Neural Networks - Sequence Models. These courses start from the basic element and cover a lot of deep learning subjects in detail. With the help of exercises and quizzes available at each course, you can make sure that you understand the subjects well. Although each course takes about four weeks to be completed, because of my prior knowledge, I was able to finish some of them in less than two days with a score of 100/100 (even optional parts) :)

Coursera


Introduction to Deep Learning (MIT)


MIT 6.S191 (Introduction to Deep Learning) is another good learning source. It's short and useful, covering essential topics in deep learning. They even went beyond the scope of common deep learning educational resources and talked about subjects like Reinforcement Learning.

Deep Learning (Book)


This book is perfect for those who want to learn deep learning from basic and for those who like mathematics. Just look at the authors, Ian Goodfellow and Yoshua Bengio (I have a bad feeling for the third one, like he was their typing machine :)). Actually, this book is the main reference for deep learning courses in university. Although it has very good content, it takes a lot of time, so I was able to read till the middle of Part II. I'm looking to finish it in my free time...
