LEARNING ADVENTURE

Changing the connections

Sometimes, having too many learning sources can be confusing - especially in deep learning. My solution? Keep diving into articles, books, and videos. Along the way, I've picked up knowledge (and likely written some code) in the following areas:

Basic: Layers (Dense, BatchNorm, Dropout, ...) - Regularization Techniques (Dropout, BatchNorm, Data augmentation, DropConnect, ...) - Loss functions (Hinge loss, Cross Entropy losses, MSE, MAE, ...) - Optimization algorithms (SGD, SGD+Momentum, Nesterov momentum, Adagrad, RMSProp, Adam) - Preprocessing
Computer Vision: Layers (Conv., TransConv., GDN, IGDN, Pooling, ...)
Recurrent Neural Networks: Layers (Simple RNN, LSTM, GRU, Conv. LSTM, Bidirectionals)
TinyML: Quantization - Pruning - Clustering - Neuron Merging - Knowledge Distillation - Early Exit Networks - NAS - ...
Generative Adversarial Networks: CGAN - DCGAN - CycleGAN - ...
Reinforcement Learning: Essentials
Network Structures: AE (AE, VAE, CAE, ...) - Classification (AlexNet, VGG, GoogLeNet, ResNe(x)t, NiN, FractalNet, DenseNet, SqueezeNet, ...) - Detection ((Fast(er)) R-CNN, SPPNet, YOLO, ...) - Segmentation (Mask R-CNN, SegNet, U-Net, ...)
Applications: Face detection and recognition - NLP and translation - Telecommunication coding - Data generation - Style transfer - Word embedding - ...
Etc.

Through this process, I've found the following sources very helpful.

CS231n (Stanford)


One of the best resources for me was the CS231n video series (Convolutional Neural Networks for Visual Recognition) from Stanford. I watched both the 2016 and 2017 versions. While Andrej Karpathy took center stage in the first, I found the 2017 course even more engaging. The explanations were beautifully intuitive - I loved it. Hats off to them!

Coursera


A well-known option is the Deep Learning Specialization by Andrew Ng on Coursera. It includes five courses: Neural Networks and Deep Learning, Improving Deep Neural Networks, Structuring Machine Learning Projects, Convolutional Neural Networks, and Sequence Models. The series starts from the basics and covers many topics in depth. With hands-on exercises and quizzes, you can truly grasp the material. Thanks to my prior knowledge, I finished some courses in under two days - with a perfect score of 100/100 (including the optional parts) :)

Coursera


Introduction to Deep Learning (MIT)


MIT's 6.S191 (Introduction to Deep Learning) is another excellent resource. It's concise yet highly informative, covering the essentials and even touching on more advanced topics like reinforcement learning.

Deep Learning (Book)


This book is perfect if you want to learn deep learning from the ground up - especially if you enjoy math. Just look at the authors: Ian Goodfellow and Yoshua Bengio (I have a weird feeling about the third author - I can't shake the sense he might have been their typing machine :). It's a core reference for many university courses. While the content is excellent, it takes time; I got halfway through Part II before life intervened. Hopefully, I'll finish it in my free time...

Deep Learning (Book)


Efficient ML (MIT)


When it comes to TinyML, who's more influential than Song Han? MIT's 6.5940 (Efficient ML) is a great way to learn the latest techniques - covering model compression, quantization, pruning, and more. A must-watch if you care about efficient models.
